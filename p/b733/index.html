What B7-33 is

B7-33 is a synthetic peptide derived from the HSP20 protein family. Research shows potent anti-fibrotic effects, particularly in cardiac and pulmonary fibrosis models. It inhibits TGF-beta signaling pathway to prevent fibrotic tissue deposition.

Mechanism

B7-33 acts as a competitive inhibitor of TGF-beta signaling by binding to the TGF-beta receptor complex. This prevents downstream fibrotic signaling, reducing collagen deposition and fibroblast activation in tissues prone to fibrosis.

Common questions

How many units is 1,000 mcg of B7-33 on an insulin syringe?

40 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 5 mg vial is reconstituted with 2 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.4 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 5 mg vial of B7-33?

2 ml is the volume used here, giving 2.5 mg per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 5 mg however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is B7-33 injected?

Daily. A typical course runs 12 weeks, which is 84 injections. Reported half-life is Unknown.

How many vials does one B7-33 cycle take?

About 17 5 mg vials for the full course - 84 doses at 1,000 mcg each, 84 mg in total.

What ARA290 (Cibinetide) is

ARA290 is a synthetic peptide that mimics the tissue-protective effects of erythropoietin (EPO) without stimulating red blood cell production. Clinical trials for neuropathic pain (small fiber neuropathy) and tissue protection. FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation.

Mechanism

ARA290 binds to the tissue-protective heteromeric receptor (EPOR/CD131) without activating the classical EPOR homodimer responsible for erythropoiesis. This provides tissue protection, nerve regeneration, and pain relief without increasing hematocrit or blood viscosity.

Reported effects and risks

  • Tissue protective effects without RBC increase
  • Neuropathic pain relief
  • Nerve regeneration support
  • No hematocrit elevation (unlike EPO)

  • General: generally well-tolerated

Administration notes

  • Subcutaneous injection daily

  • Can combine with other nerve support
  • Monitor pain levels and nerve function

Common questions

How many units is 4,000 mcg of ARA290 (Cibinetide) on an insulin syringe?

40 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 10 mg vial is reconstituted with 1 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.4 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 10 mg vial of ARA290 (Cibinetide)?

1 ml is the volume used here, giving 10 mg per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 10 mg however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is ARA290 (Cibinetide) injected?

Daily or 3x weekly. A typical course runs 12 weeks, which is 36-84 injections depending where in that range you dose. Reported half-life is Unknown.

How many vials does one ARA290 (Cibinetide) cycle take?

15-34 10 mg vials for the full course - 36-84 doses at 4,000 mcg each.

Is ARA290 (Cibinetide) approved for human use?

Human trial data. Published human clinical trials exist at comparable doses. The compound is not approved for this use, and trial doses were given under supervision.

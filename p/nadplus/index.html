What NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is

NAD+ is an essential coenzyme found in every living cell. Declines 50% by age 50. Essential for mitochondrial function, DNA repair via PARP enzymes, and sirtuin activation (longevity genes). IV NAD+ has shown benefits in addiction recovery, chronic fatigue, and anti-aging.

Mechanism

NAD+ is the oxidized form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. Required for cellular respiration (mitochondrial ATP production), DNA repair enzyme activation (PARPs), and sirtuin deacetylase activity. Higher NAD+ levels support metabolic health, genomic stability, and cellular stress resistance.

Reported effects and risks

Reported benefits

  • Essential coenzyme for cellular energy (ATP production)
  • Activates sirtuins (longevity genes)
  • Supports DNA repair mechanisms (PARP enzymes)
  • Declines with age - supplementation restores levels
  • Improves mitochondrial function
  • Shown benefits in addiction recovery
  • May enhance metabolic health

Reported drawbacks

  • Subcutaneous less common than IV
  • Large doses needed (500-2000mcg)
  • Effects may take weeks to notice
  • Expensive for long-term use
  • Oral NR/NMN may be more practical for some
  • Individual response varies significantly

Warnings

  • Cancer: high NAD+ may theoretically support cancer metabolism
  • Autoimmune: immune modulating effects
  • Methylation: high doses may deplete methyl groups
  • Pregnancy: insufficient data
  • General: monitor for excessive energy/stimulation

Administration notes

  • Subcutaneous injection (IV more common but invasive)
  • Daily dosing or 3x weekly maintenance
  • Morning dosing may improve energy
  • Large injection volume - split doses if needed
  • Combine with TMG or methyl donors
  • Cycling optional but continuous OK

Common questions

How many units is 250 mg of NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) on an insulin syringe?

50 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 500 mg vial is reconstituted with 1 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.5 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 500 mg vial of NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide)?

1 ml is the volume used here, giving 500 mg per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 500 mg however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

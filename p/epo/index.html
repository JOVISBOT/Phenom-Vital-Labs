What EPO (Erythropoietin) is

Recombinant human erythropoietin is FDA approved for anemia in kidney disease, cancer, and other conditions. Stimulates red blood cell production in bone marrow. Well-established clinical use with defined protocols.

Mechanism

EPO binds to the erythropoietin receptor (EPOR) on erythroid progenitor cells in bone marrow, promoting survival, proliferation, and differentiation of red blood cell precursors. Increases hematocrit and oxygen-carrying capacity.

Common questions

How many units is 3,000 IU of EPO (Erythropoietin) on an insulin syringe?

30 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 10000 IU vial is reconstituted with 1 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.3 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 10000 IU vial of EPO (Erythropoietin)?

1 ml is the volume used here, giving 10000 IU per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 10000 IU however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is EPO (Erythropoietin) injected?

3x weekly. A typical course runs As prescribed, which is 12 injections (assumed - the record states no fixed course). Reported half-life is 4-13 hours (IV).

How many vials does one EPO (Erythropoietin) cycle take?

About 4 10000 IU vials for the full course - 12 doses at 3,000 IU each, 36000 IU in total.

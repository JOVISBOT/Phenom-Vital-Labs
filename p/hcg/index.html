What HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin) is

HCG is FDA approved for fertility treatments and hypogonadism. Structurally similar to LH, it stimulates Leydig cells to produce testosterone and maintains testicular function. Widely used in TRT and PCT protocols.

Mechanism

HCG binds to LH receptors on Leydig cells in the testes, stimulating testosterone production and maintaining testicular size and function. Unlike exogenous testosterone, it preserves fertility and natural hormone axis while supporting testosterone levels.

Common questions

How many units is 500 IU of HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin) on an insulin syringe?

30 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 5000 IU vial is reconstituted with 3 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.3 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 5000 IU vial of HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin)?

3 ml is the volume used here, giving 1666.67 IU per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 5000 IU however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin) injected?

3x weekly. A typical course runs As needed, which is 12 injections. Reported half-life is 24-36 hours.

How many vials does one HCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin) cycle take?

About 2 5000 IU vials for the full course - 12 doses at 500 IU each, 6000 IU in total.

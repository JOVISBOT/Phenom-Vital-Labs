What DSIP is

Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide discovered in 1963. Research shows promotion of slow-wave (deep) sleep without sedation or hangover. May modulate stress hormones and improve recovery. Studied for insomnia, withdrawal, and stress-related conditions.

Mechanism

DSIP crosses the blood-brain barrier and modulates sleep-wake cycles by interacting with GABAergic and monoaminergic systems. It increases slow-wave sleep (stages 3-4) without altering REM sleep. Also influences ACTH/cortisol and may have anti-stress effects.

Common questions

How many units is 200 mcg of DSIP on an insulin syringe?

12 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 5 mg vial is reconstituted with 3 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.12 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 5 mg vial of DSIP?

3 ml is the volume used here, giving 1.67 mg per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 5 mg however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is DSIP injected?

Daily at bedtime. A typical course runs As needed, which is 28 injections (assumed - the record states no fixed course). Reported half-life is Unknown (short).

How many vials does one DSIP cycle take?

About 2 5 mg vials for the full course - 28 doses at 200 mcg each, 5.6 mg in total.

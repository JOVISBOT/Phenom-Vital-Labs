What MOTS-C is

MOTS-C is a mitochondrial-derived peptide encoded by mitochondrial DNA. Discovered in 2015, research shows it regulates metabolism, improves insulin sensitivity, and may protect against metabolic syndrome and age-related decline.

Mechanism

MOTS-C is imported from mitochondria to nucleus where it regulates gene expression for metabolic homeostasis. It activates AMPK (cellular energy sensor), improving glucose uptake and fatty acid oxidation. Acts as a mitochondrial signaling molecule.

Common questions

How many units is 5 mg of MOTS-C on an insulin syringe?

50 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 10 mg vial is reconstituted with 1 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.5 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 10 mg vial of MOTS-C?

1 ml is the volume used here, giving 10 mg per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 10 mg however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is MOTS-C injected?

2-3x weekly. A typical course runs 4 on, 4 off, which is 8-12 injections depending where in that range you dose. Reported half-life is Unknown.

How many vials does one MOTS-C cycle take?

4-6 10 mg vials for the full course - 8-12 doses at 5 mg each.

What Dihexa is

Dihexa is a synthetic hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) mimetic developed at Washington State University. Research shows 7x more potent than BDNF for synaptogenesis. Studies demonstrate enhanced memory formation, cognitive performance, and potential neuroprotective effects.

Mechanism

Dihexa crosses the blood-brain barrier and activates the HGF/c-MET pathway, promoting synaptogenesis (formation of new synapses) and neurogenesis. It enhances dendritic arborization and has shown 7x the synapse-forming activity of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF).

Common questions

How many units is 3,000 mcg of Dihexa on an insulin syringe?

15 units on a U-100 insulin syringe, when a 20 mg vial is reconstituted with 1 ml of bacteriostatic water. That is 0.15 ml. A U-100 syringe reads 100 units per millilitre whether the barrel holds 30, 50 or 100 units - the barrel size changes how much fits, not what a unit means.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a 20 mg vial of Dihexa?

1 ml is the volume used here, giving 20 mg per ml. Water changes the concentration, not the amount of peptide: the vial holds 20 mg however much you add. More water means a larger, easier-to-read draw for the same dose.

How often is Dihexa injected?

Daily. A typical course runs 4-6 weeks, which is 28-42 injections depending where in that range you dose. Reported half-life is Unknown.

How many vials does one Dihexa cycle take?

5-7 20 mg vials for the full course - 28-42 doses at 3,000 mcg each.
